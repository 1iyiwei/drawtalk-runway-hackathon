# Logistics

Since this is your very first hackathon, welcome to a highly energetic, fast-paced day! Because it runs alongside the Runway AI Summit, you will be surrounded by a strong mix of developers, researchers, and creators.
Based on your confirmed registration, the official schedule, and constraints, here is a detailed breakdown of what to expect, what to prep ahead of time, and how to execute on-site.

------------------------------
## ⏱️ What the Day Will Look Like
The timeline for this event is exceptionally tight, meaning every hour counts.

* 8:30 AM – 9:00 AM | Arrival & Setup: Doors open at The Masonic. You'll check in, grab your badge, find a workspace, plug into the Wi-Fi, and get your dev environment ready. [2]
* 9:00 AM | Kickoff: Official opening remarks. Runway, AWS, and NVIDIA engineering teams will introduce the APIs, available models, compute resources, and prompt guidelines.
* 9:30 AM – 3:30 PM | The Deep Work Sprint (6 Hours): This is the core building window. You will code, test, and iterate rapidly. Lunch will be provided on-site so you don't have to leave the venue and lose momentum. [2]
* 3:30 PM | Firm Submission Deadline: All projects must be submitted to the hackathon platform. No exceptions. [2]
* 3:30 PM – 5:00 PM | Science Fair & Judging: Judges will review the submissions. Teams often set up "science fair" style tables to show off working live demos.
* 5:00 PM | Awards & Happy Hour: Winners are announced, followed by a celebration network mixer to mingle with colleagues in person. [2]

------------------------------
## 📋 What to Prepare BEFORE Hand (Pre-Hackathon)
To maximize your 6-hour building window, you should eliminate all administrative and setup friction before you step off your flight from Toronto.

* Setup Accounts & API Access: You are explicitly required to create a Runway Developer Account before arriving. Ensure your API keys are generated and working. [2]
* Prepare Your Core Scaffold: Do not start coding from scratch on Wednesday morning. Prepare a "skeleton" repository on GitHub containing your boilerplate code—such as your backend server framework, API call handlers, and basic UI layouts.
* Formulate Your Architecture: You've previously noted an interest in a "Multi-modal movie maker" that handles text-to-story creation and direct manipulation for motion, paths, and scenes. Draft your architecture diagrams now. Decide exactly what tools you are using (e.g., Python, LangGraph for your agentic workflow, or a local frontend interface). [1]
* Pack the Essentials: Bring your laptop, charger, a reliable extension cord/power strip (outlets fill up fast!), and notebooks for quick sketching.

------------------------------
## 🚀 What Has to Be Done ON-SITE
Once you are in the room, your primary objective is to manage scope and build a viable product.

* Establish a "Minimum Viable Product" (MVP) by 12:00 PM: Focus entirely on your core feature first. For your creative video tool idea, make sure a basic prompt can hit the Runway API and return an animated video sequence reliably before adding complex path manipulation features.
* Utilize On-Site Support: Major engineers from Runway, AWS, and NVIDIA will be walking the floor. If you hit a rate limit, an obscure API error code, or infrastructure issues, ask them immediately instead of spending an hour debugging solo.
* Build a Compelling 2-Minute Demo: Judges rarely look deeply at your codebase; they care about the end product and user experience. Dedicate the final hour (2:30 PM – 3:30 PM) to recording a flawless, screen-recorded video demo of your tool working. If your live deployment fails during judging due to clogged venue Wi-Fi, a pre-recorded backup video will save your submission.
